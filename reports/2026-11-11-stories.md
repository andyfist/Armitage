### Lot 764: Pair of Chinese cloisonne vases

Cloisonne reached China via the Silk Road in the Yuan dynasty and became an imperial art by the 15th century (the 'Jingtai blue' period); under Qianlong in the 18th century the wirework became finer and more painterly, and many later 19th-century pieces carry apocryphal reign marks, so a small dented pair is more likely later export ware than palace quality. ### Lot 791: Small oil painting 'King River Tasmania' by Janet Morley
Closes 4:23:20 pm. Bid $0. No usable sold price yet. [Lot page](https://armitage.bidsonline.com.au/catalogue_detail.aspx?cdid=3111053&chid=28975&category=ALL&style=group)

The King River runs 52 km from the West Coast Range to Macquarie Harbour near Strahan and was once called Australia's most polluted river: from the 1880s the Mount Lyell copper mine at Queenstown dumped waste and, from 1922 to 1995, about 1.5 million tonnes of tailings a year into the Queen/King system, building a delta of around 100 million tonnes in the harbour. Its upper valley was drowned by the Crotty Dam (Lake Burbury) in 1992, and the old Abt rack railway ran along the lower river. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_River_(Tasmania)
