# Gowans 368: why most books were not on the Chrome list, and what was checked on 22 Oct 2026

## Why only 3 books got Chrome research

Every book lot in the sale (about 50) was looked at. They all went through the photo scan: books are an "always look" category, so the title filter never threw any of them out. The cut came one step later, at the profit rule.

The Chrome list only takes lots that look able to make **$200 profit after the 19.8% buyer's premium and about 15% selling fees**. For a box of books that means the books inside have to resell for about **$265 together even if the box costs only $20**. Gowans' own history shows how rarely that happens:

- 2,652 book and comic lots sold at Gowans from July 2024 to October 2026. **25 made $250 or more** (about 1 in 100). 235 made $100 or more.
- 1,325 of them were boxes or tubs of books: median **$25**, 9 in 10 under $80. The best was $1,200 (three boxes of vintage natural history books).
- Even lots Gowans labelled "1st edition" (12 of them) had a median of $52; the best was $230 (Hemingway, Men Without Women, 1927).

## Why "any book could be a first edition" mostly doesn't pay

You're right that any book could be a first edition. But a first edition is only worth money when the book was **wanted and printed in small numbers when it first came out**. A first edition of a Danielle Steel, a Bryce Courtenay or a Reader's Digest book was printed by the hundred thousand and sells for a few dollars. The first editions worth money are things like early Australian books, classic authors' first books, famous children's books (Harry Potter, Roald Dahl) in their first printing, and signed or limited copies.

The other problem is that **the photos can't show the edition**. You find a first edition on the copyright page inside the book, and in a box photo you only see spines. Chrome research can't see any more than the photos show: it can only look up prices for titles we can read. So the honest way to catch a first edition in a box is: pick out the titles that would be valuable *if* they were firsts, price them, and then check the copyright pages at the viewing.

## What to check at the Friday-morning viewing (8:30 to 9:30am)

- Copyright page (back of the title page): "First published 19xx" with no later reprint dates listed.
- Dust jacket present, and its price still on the front flap (not clipped).
- Library stamps, pockets or spine labels (these cut the value a lot).
- Signatures or inscriptions by the author.
- For lot 715: the back of the title page. A 1988 date means facsimile.
